Why Used Furniture Is Often the Better Buy

  • Better construction for the money. A lot of new budget furniture is particleboard with a printed finish. Older pieces from established makers are often solid hardwood or quality veneer over hardwood with dovetailed drawers, the kind of build that costs a fortune new.
  • No waiting. No backorders, multi-week lead times or delivery windows. You see it, buy it and take it home.
  • One of a kind. Your living room won't look like the catalog page everyone else ordered from.
  • It's already “broken in.” Real wood that's lasted 30 years has proven itself. It isn't going to fall apart in three.
  • Your money stays local. At a consignment shop, part of every sale goes back to the neighbor who consigned the piece.

How to Inspect Used Furniture Before You Buy

Wood furniture (dressers, tables, cabinets)

  • Pull every drawer. It should glide without sticking. Look at the corners: dovetail joints mean quality; staples and glue usually mean the opposite.
  • Push on it. A table or chair that wobbles may just need tightening, but a cracked leg or split joint is a bigger job.
  • Check the back and underside. Solid wood or plywood backs are a good sign. Thin cardboard-like backing and particleboard aren't.
  • Look for water damage. White rings are usually surface-level and fixable. Swollen, bubbled veneer is not.

Upholstered furniture (sofas, chairs, headboards)

  • Smell it. Smoke, pet and mildew odors are very hard to remove from upholstery. In our humid Lowcountry climate, a musty smell is a hard pass.
  • Lift a corner. A heavy frame usually means hardwood. If it flexes or creaks, the frame may be weak.
  • Sit on every cushion. Feel for sagging springs or bottomed-out foam.
  • Check seams, piping and tufts for pests. Tiny dark spots or shed skins in the seams are a warning sign. Buy from sellers who inspect what they put out, and never bring home curbside upholstery.

Measure everything

Measure the room, the doorways, the hallway and any stair turns before you shop, and keep the numbers on your phone. A tape measure in the car has saved plenty of shoppers from buying a sectional that won't go through the front door.

Solid Wood, Veneer or Particleboard? How to Tell

  • Solid wood is heavy, and the grain on the edge or end of a board matches the grain on top.
  • Quality veneer is a thin layer of real wood over a solid or plywood core. It isn't a bad thing: much of the best mid-century furniture is veneered. Real veneer has natural grain that never repeats exactly.
  • Particleboard or MDF with laminate is lighter than it looks, often has a printed “wood” pattern that repeats, and shows crumbly edges where it's chipped.

Tips for Getting the Best Used Furniture

  • Shop often. Inventory at a consignment store changes daily. The great pieces are gone in days, sometimes hours.
  • Follow new arrivals. We post new pieces on Facebook and Instagram, so following is the easiest way to see things first.
  • Buy the big pieces first. Anchor the room with a sofa, a table or a bed, then fill in the lamps, mirrors and art. Our budget decorating guide walks through it room by room.
  • Ask questions. Where did it come from? Any repairs? A good shop will tell you honestly.
  • Plan the pickup. Bring a truck or a friend with one, or ask about delivery options for larger purchases.
