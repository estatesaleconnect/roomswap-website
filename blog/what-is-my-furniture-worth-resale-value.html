What Is My Used Furniture Worth? A Realistic Guide to Resale Value

Straight talk from a shop that prices furniture every day: the rule of thumb, real price ranges, and why that $2,000 sofa won't bring $1,500.

The Short Answer

As a rule of thumb, used furniture in good condition resells for about 30–50% of what a comparable piece would cost new today, and about 15–30% if it shows visible wear. Solid wood, known brands, mid-century and antique pieces can bring more; particleboard, stains and odors bring less. At Room Swap, consignors receive 40% of the final sale price. For a real number on your piece, text photos to 843-900-1412 for a free evaluation.

“What's this worth?” is the question we hear most at Room Swap, usually with a photo of a sofa, a dining set or a dresser that's been in the family for years. The honest answer is almost always “less than you paid, but more than you'd think,” and the details depend on a handful of things you can check yourself.

Here's how we think about furniture resale value at our Holly Hill showroom, with real price ranges from our market in Orangeburg County and the SC Lowcountry.

The Rule of Thumb: 30–50% of Replacement Cost

Most everyday used furniture in good condition sells for roughly 30–50% of its replacement cost, meaning what a comparable new piece would cost at a store today. Pieces with visible wear usually land at 15–30%.

Note that it's replacement cost, not what you originally paid. A sofa bought for $1,800 in 2012 is compared with what a similar new sofa costs now, and then adjusted for age, condition and demand.

Why so much less than new? Buyers of used furniture are trading the new-store experience (delivery, warranty, choosing the color) for a lower price. Furniture also depreciates like a car: the moment it leaves the showroom, it's “used.” And in a price-sensitive market like ours, fairly priced pieces sell in days while overpriced ones sit for months.

What Raises Furniture Value

  • Solid wood construction, dovetailed drawers and heavy, sturdy frames
  • Known brands such as Ethan Allen, La-Z-Boy and Herman Miller, plus quality Southern makers like Thomasville and Henredon
  • Mid-century modern and true antiques in good condition
  • Complete sets, like a dining table with all its chairs and leaves or a dresser with its mirror
  • Neutral, current upholstery that's clean and odor-free
  • Versatile sizes. Apartment-scale sofas and compact dining sets fit more homes.
  • Good timing. Indoor furniture demand rises in the fall, and porch and patio pieces sell best in spring.

What Lowers Furniture Value

  • Particleboard and laminate, especially once edges chip or swell
  • Odors from smoke, pets or mildew, which are very hard to remove from upholstery
  • Stains, tears and pet damage
  • Missing parts: hardware, shelves, table leaves or a chair from the set
  • Dated, oversized styles, like huge entertainment centers built for tube TVs and very bulky overstuffed sofas

A little cleaning and a few minor repairs can move a piece up a whole condition tier. Here's how to prepare furniture for consignment.

What We Can't Take

For health, safety and resale reasons, we don't accept mattresses, car seats, cribs, helmets, used appliances, outdated electronics, china hutches, incomplete furniture sets, or items with significant damage, stains, mold, pet damage or strong odors.

“But I Paid So Much for It!”

We get it, and we're always honest about it. What a piece cost years ago doesn't set what a buyer will pay today. The good news is that quality furniture that's priced right sells, often within a few days to a few weeks, so it turns into money instead of sitting in the garage. And if a piece isn't a fit for us, we'll tell you so rather than waste your time.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is my used couch worth?

In our market, a sofa in good condition typically sells for about $75 to $175, a loveseat for $50 to $125, and a large name-brand sectional in excellent condition for about $250 to $450. Brand, age, fabric, odor and wear move the price. Text photos to 843-900-1412 for a specific evaluation.

How do I find out what my furniture is worth?

Start with the rule of thumb: good-condition used furniture usually resells for 30 to 50 percent of what a comparable new piece costs today. For a real number, send clear photos, brand labels, measurements and close-ups of any damage to a local consignment shop. Room Swap gives free evaluations by text at 843-900-1412.

Is old furniture worth anything?

Often, yes. Solid wood furniture, pieces from quality makers, mid-century modern furniture and true antiques can be worth more than newer budget furniture. Particleboard furniture and pieces with heavy damage or odors usually have little resale value.

How much does a consignment store pay for furniture?

Consignment splits vary by store. Room Swap Consignments pays consignors 40% of the final sale price when the item sells, by check, Venmo or PayPal. For example, a dresser that sells for $150 pays the consignor $60.

Does Room Swap buy furniture outright?

Yes. Room Swap buys quality furniture, estate contents, and gold, silver and jewelry outright, with same-day payment available. Outright offers are lower than consignment sale prices because the store takes on the risk and the wait.
