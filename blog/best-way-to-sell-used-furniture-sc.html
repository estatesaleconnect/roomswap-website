The Best Way to Sell Used Furniture in South Carolina: Marketplace vs. Consignment vs. Selling Outright

Fastest, easiest or most money? Each way to sell furniture has a trade-off. Here's an honest side-by-side so you can pick the right one.

Option 1: Facebook Marketplace and Craigslist

Selling privately can bring the highest price, since you keep 100% of what the buyer pays. But anyone who's tried it knows the hidden costs.

  • The time. Photos, a listing, answering “is this still available?” 40 times, haggling, rescheduling.
  • No-shows and lowballers. Plenty of buyers commit and never come, or show up and offer half.
  • Scams. Common ones include fake payment screenshots, “overpayment” checks, and requests for a verification code sent to your phone. Never share a code, and only accept cash or payment you've confirmed has arrived.
  • Strangers at your house. Furniture is too big to meet in a parking lot, so buyers come to you. Have someone with you and move the piece to the garage or porch beforehand.
  • The lifting. You'll be helping carry that dresser down the stairs.

Marketplace makes the most sense for a single, easy-to-move, in-demand item and a seller who has time to manage it.

Option 3: Sell Outright for Cash

If you need the furniture gone and the money now, selling outright is the cleanest option. We buy quality furniture, estate contents, and gold, silver and jewelry directly, with same-day payment available.

An outright offer is lower than what the same piece might eventually sell for on consignment, because the store takes on the risk, the floor space and the wait. In return, you're done the same day. It's a great fit when you're moving on a deadline, clearing a rental, or selling pieces we know will move quickly.

Option 4: Yard Sales

Yard sales are good for clearing out small household items, and a Lowcountry summer Saturday at 7 AM is a rite of passage. But they're a poor way to sell furniture. Shoppers come expecting yard-sale prices, a quality dresser often goes for a fraction of what it would bring in a showroom, and whatever doesn't sell has to be dragged back inside.

Option 5: A Whole House? Estate Sale or Buyout

When it's an entire home (settling an estate, downsizing a parent, or moving out of state), selling piece by piece isn't realistic. What Sells Best, and What Doesn't

Sells well

  • Solid wood dining tables and chairs, dressers, chests and nightstands
  • Clean, comfortable sofas and sectionals in neutral colors
  • Name brands such as Ethan Allen, La-Z-Boy and Herman Miller
  • Mid-century modern and true antique pieces
  • Accent chairs, bar carts, mirrors, lamps and quality decor
  • Porch and patio furniture, especially in spring

Harder to sell

  • Particleboard and laminate furniture
  • Bulky entertainment centers built for old tube TVs
  • Upholstery with stains, pet damage or smoke odor
  • Incomplete sets

We don't accept mattresses, china hutches, used appliances or outdated electronics.

How to Get the Most Money for Your Furniture

  • Clean it. A wipe-down, a vacuum and polished hardware make a real difference. See how to prepare furniture for consignment.
  • Take good photos in daylight, straight-on, with close-ups of any brand label and any damage.
  • Gather the details: brand, measurements, age and the original receipt if you have it.
  • Keep sets together. A table with all its chairs and leaves is worth more than the pieces separately.
  • Be realistic. Most used furniture resells for a fraction of what it cost new. Our furniture resale value guide explains what to expect.
  • Mind the season. Indoor furniture demand rises in the fall as people get ready to host for the holidays, and patio furniture sells best in spring.
