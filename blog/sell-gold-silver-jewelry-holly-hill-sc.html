How to Tell What You Have: Reading Hallmarks

Most gold and silver is stamped with a small mark showing its purity, usually on a ring's inner band, near a necklace clasp, or on the back of flatware handles. A jeweler's loupe or your phone's camera zoom helps.

A quick home check: gold and silver aren't magnetic. If a strong magnet grabs the whole piece, it's likely plated base metal. (A small steel spring in a clasp can be magnetic even on a real chain.) Don't try scratch or acid tests at home. You can damage the piece, and a buyer will test it properly anyway.

How Gold and Silver Buyers Set a Price

For scrap gold and silver, the starting point is melt value: the weight of the item, times its purity, times the day's “spot” price for the metal.

Melt value, step by step

Precious metals are priced per troy ounce, which is 31.1 grams (a little heavier than a kitchen ounce). Jewelers often weigh in grams or pennyweight (1 dwt = 1.555 grams).

Example: a 10-gram 14K chain contains 10 × 0.585 = 5.85 grams of pure gold, or about 0.19 troy ounces. Multiply that by the day's gold spot price and you have its melt value.

No buyer pays 100% of melt value for scrap, because refining costs money and prices move every day. What matters is that the offer is clear, the weighing happens in front of you, and you understand what you're being paid for.

When a piece is worth more than its metal

Some jewelry is worth more as jewelry than as scrap: antique and Victorian pieces, signed designer jewelry, fine craftsmanship, and pieces with quality stones. Because Room Swap also sells jewelry in our showroom, we look at each piece both ways, as metal and as something a customer would love to wear, instead of treating everything as melt.

7 Tips for Getting a Fair Price

  1. Check the spot price the day you sell. Any major precious-metals price site lists live gold and silver prices. You'll know whether an offer is in the right ballpark.
  2. Sort by karat. Group your 10K, 14K and 18K pieces, and keep sterling separate from silverplate. It makes the evaluation faster and more transparent.
  3. Watch the weighing. A reputable buyer weighs your items on a scale you can see and explains how they arrived at the number.
  4. Ask whether it's a melt offer. If a piece is antique, signed or set with good stones, ask whether that was factored in.
  5. Don't over-clean. Polishing antique silver or scrubbing old jewelry can strip patina or loosen stones. Bring it as it is.
  6. Be cautious with mail-in kits and pop-up buyers. Consumer advocates have long warned that mailing jewelry to a buyer you've never met, or selling at a one-day hotel event, leaves you with little recourse. A local shop with a permanent address gives you an in-person offer you can walk away from.
  7. Bring a photo ID. Reputable buyers record seller identification on precious-metal purchases, as South Carolina law requires.

Selling Sterling Silver Flatware and Tea Sets

Inherited sterling is one of the most common things families ask us about. A few things to know:

  • Check every piece. Sets are often mixed. Look for “Sterling” or “925” on the back of each handle.
  • Knives are different. Most sterling knives have hollow handles filled with a cement-like material and stainless steel blades, so only part of the knife is silver.
  • Weighted pieces like candlesticks and some bowls are filled at the base, so they contain less silver than their weight suggests.
  • Silverplate isn't worthless. Plated tea sets, trays and serving pieces have little metal value, but pretty pieces still sell for entertaining and holiday decor. (See our vintage Christmas decor guide for how shoppers use them.)

Inherited Jewelry: Take Your Time

If you're settling a loved one's estate, don't feel rushed to sell everything at once. Set aside pieces with sentimental value first, have the rest evaluated, and decide from there. How much will I get for 14K gold?

14K gold is 58.5% pure gold. Its melt value is the weight in grams times 0.585 times the day's gold spot price per gram. Buyers pay a percentage of that melt value, and antique, signed or gemstone pieces can be worth more than their metal.

Do you buy broken jewelry?

Yes. Broken chains, single earrings, bent rings and damaged pieces still have precious-metal value. Bring them in with the rest of your jewelry.

Is silverplate worth anything?

Silverplate has very little metal value because the silver layer is thin, but attractive plated tea sets, trays and serving pieces can still sell as decor and for entertaining.
